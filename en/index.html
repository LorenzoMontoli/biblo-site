With Biblo you can

The work that takes up most of the time in research, done on your own documents, with the sources always in view.

Your whole library, with every question

You don't upload files to the chat or pick, each time, which documents to work on: Biblo has already read everything you've connected and, for every question, finds the passages it needs across the whole library on its own. That makes it an assistant built around your work and what you're trying to get done. And if you want to focus on a few texts, a selection narrows the field.

Question your bibliography

Ask your PDFs questions in plain language: the answer comes with the passages it quotes, document and page.

Find the texts on a topic

Describe a subject and Biblo tells you which volumes really discuss it, ranked by relevance.

Summarise, analyse, compare

Summarising a volume, analysing an argument, comparing two texts: each task has its own specialised assistant.

Relate your sources

Biblo builds cards for authors, works and figures and links your documents to each other, so you see where they meet.

Rebuild the citations

A single index of everything your texts cite (works, authors, figures) with how often each one appears.

Work on scans too

PDFs without text, such as scanned volumes, are read with OCR and can be questioned like the others.

A help, not a replacement

Biblo doesn't write your research for you: it reads, searches and lines up the sources. Every answer can be checked against the text, and if the answer isn't in your documents, it says so. The questions, the choices and the conclusions remain yours.

Free, and you're in control

Biblo costs nothing and has no subscription. For the AI it uses your own personal Google Gemini key, which is free too: it takes a minute to create and the app shows you how. Requests go through your account, your files stay on your computer (Google only receives the passage needed for each answer), and you can revoke the key whenever you like.

Keyword search finds the word, not the answer

Once you have hundreds of volumes, the problem is no longer finding a term: it's remembering which book discussed it, and how. Biblo holds both ends — it searches meaning rather than letters, and hands you the exact passage its answer came from.

Searches by meaning

Ask “how was consent justified” and it finds the passages about it, even when those exact words never appear.

Shows its evidence

Under every answer sit the files it used and the passages it quoted, with page numbers. One click opens the PDF at the right spot.

Admits what it doesn't know

If the answer isn't in your documents, it says so instead of inventing one.

How it works

Three steps, once

After that the library stays ready: it only updates when you add new documents.

1

Point it at your folders

Tell Biblo where the PDFs are. They stay exactly where they are: no uploads, no copies in the cloud, no folders to reorganise.

2

Biblo reads, inspects, connects

Three separate, repeatable phases. Read prepares the text for search. Inspect builds the cards for authors, works and figures. Find connections relates the documents to one another.

3

Ask

Type your question in the chat. Biblo picks the right assistant, searches the texts, selects the passages and answers with citations.

Privacy

Your documents never leave your computer

Reading, the index and the search store are local: no file is uploaded anywhere. When you ask a question, only the passage needed to answer it travels to the language model, together with your question. It doesn't pass through servers of ours, and we don't keep it.

No uploads

The PDFs stay in your folders. The index lives next to them, on your disk.

Only what's needed

What leaves is the passage required for that single answer, not the library.

Your own key

Biblo uses your key with the model provider: requests go through your account, not ours.

What you need

Windows 10 or 11, or a Mac

64-bit Windows, or a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 15 Sequoia or later. There are no builds for Linux or for Intel-based Macs for now.

Disk space

Around 2.5 GB: the application, the model that turns text into numbers, and the index of your library.

A free AI key

From Google Gemini: it takes a minute to get one from Google's site. Without a key the AI side can't work, but everything else can: importing, reading and searching your documents all happen locally, and the app only asks for the key when you use an AI feature (chat, document inspection, connections).

No graphics card

Everything runs on the processor. On modest machines the first read is slow, but the app deliberately leaves cores free so your computer stays usable.

Questions

Do I need an internet connection?

Not to read and search your documents: that part is entirely local. You need one for the chat and for inspection, because answers and cards are generated by a language model that lives online.

What does it cost?

The application is free. For the AI features you need a key from the model provider, Google Gemini, which has a free plan: chat usage counts against that plan, not against us.

How do I get the Google Gemini key?

From Google AI Studio, with any Google account: it's free and takes a couple of minutes. The first time, Google asks you to accept its terms (you must be 18 or older); then you create the key and paste it into Biblo, which checks it. The video shows every step.

Which documents can it read?

PDFs, including scanned ones: if a document has no text layer, Biblo runs OCR on it and then treats it like any other.

How many documents can it handle?

Biblo is developed against a library of 70 volumes and over 11,000 pages, and handles it comfortably. The practical limit isn't the number of files but the time of the first read, which depends on your processor.

Windows says the publisher is unknown — is it safe?

It only happens with the .exe installer from this site: that's the warning Windows shows for any program not signed with a commercial certificate. From the Microsoft Store, Biblo installs without warnings, because Microsoft signs the package. If you use the installer, you can check the SHA-256 fingerprint of the file you downloaded against the one published above: if they match, the file is exactly the one that was released.

Are my documents used to train an AI?

Not by us: we never receive or store your files. When an answer is generated, only the necessary passage is sent to the model provider. On the free plan, though, Google may use those texts to improve its models, and there is no way to turn this off. The app explains this when you enter your key.

Can I use my own key?

Yes, and that's the normal way it works: you enter the key and requests go through your account with the provider.

If I uninstall, do I lose my documents?

No. The PDFs stay exactly where they always were, in your folders. On a Mac you remove Biblo by dragging it to the Trash; the data it produced (index, chats, settings) stays in your user's Library › Application Support › Biblo folder: delete that too if you no longer need it.
